# Research 02 — Books & Courses Cold Call

> Synthese de la litterature moderne sur le cold call B2B, optimisee pour ecrire des scripts de cold call pour agences/consultants high-ticket (l'agence et clients similaires : fondateurs d'agences B2B, conseil, services 6k EUR+). Pas de fluff — chaque section est consommable par un agent IA scripteur.

---

## TL;DR (5 lignes max)

1. **Context-first openers > permission-only openers** : la regle 2024-2026 (30MPC) — montrer en 5 secondes pourquoi *ce* prospect specifique, pas un humain au hasard. Permission seule sonne SDR generique.
2. **"Problem Proposition" > "Value Proposition"** : ouvrir sur une douleur ultra-specifique ("dangerous specificity") ; le prospect doit penser "ouch, c'est exactement mon cas".
3. **Objection = reaction a l'interruption, pas a l'offre** : la sequence universelle = Agree -> Disarm -> Reopen avec une question incitative (Mr. Miyagi de 30MPC, Validate-Pivot de Braun, mirror-label de Voss).
4. **Vendre le test drive, pas le produit** : l'ask en fin de cold call doit etre minuscule ("15 min mardi pour voir si pertinent"), jamais une demo ou une vente directe.
5. **Benchmarks 2025** : connect rate 5-12% (top quartile 13%+), set rate 5% moyen / 16% top, ratio 1 meeting pour 3 connects pour le top 10%. Volume cible 155 dials/semaine. (Source : Gong + 30MPC sur 300M de cold calls.)

## Source 1 — Cold Calling Sucks (And That's Why It Works) / Armand Farrokh & Nick Cegelski (2024)

La reference moderne. Co-fondateurs de **30 Minutes to President's Club (30MPC)**, podcast #1 sales, donnees Gong sur 300M de cold calls. Toute la suite du doc s'aligne ou se contraste avec eux.

### Framework principal

Acronyme : **Opener -> Problem Proposition -> Mr. Miyagi (objection) -> Advance (CTA)**. Cible : top 10% qui bookent 1 meeting pour 3 connects.

### Structure de script

**Phase 1 — Opener (15-30 sec)** : "context-first", deux variantes seulement.

1. **Heard the Name Tossed Around Opener** (style Armand) — leverage une connexion mutuelle (investisseurs partages, clients voisins, ecosysteme).
   - Etape 1 : lead avec familiarite ("I worked with a few of your partners…")
   - Etape 2 : intro nom
   - Etape 3 : "Have you heard our name tossed around?"
   - Si NO : "Ha! Guess I'm not as popular as I thought. Well, the reason for my call is…"
   - Si YES : "Oh! Well, we'd normally be working with someone like you by now. I guess, what'd you hear?"

2. **Tailored Permission Opener** (style Nick) — leverage une recherche specifique sur la personne.
   - "Hey Emma, this is Nick Cegelski with 30MPC. Look, I know you didn't expect me to call you this afternoon. Do you mind if I take one minute? I'll tell you why I called, and then you can tell me whether or not it's worth a chat."
   - Hook personnalise apres : press release, job posting, podcast, levee de fonds.

**Phase 2 — Problem Proposition (30-45 sec)** : remplace toute "value prop".

- Etape A : **Triggering Problem** — decrire la situation crap avec une specificite dangereuse ("Who was there? Where were they? What challenge? How were they feeling?"). Visuel concret.
- Etape B : **One-Sentence Solution** — format : "we do X so that the problem goes away" — un seul differenciateur, pas trois.
- Etape C : **Interest-Based CTA** — softeners : "Are you even moderately interested?" / "I'm guessing you're all set. But open to taking a look so you know what's out there?"

**Phase 3 — Mr. Miyagi (objection)** : 3 etapes (voir section dediee plus bas).

**Phase 4 — Advance** : sell the test drive, pas le produit. Ask minuscule ("15 min", "directional sense", "so you know what's out there").

### Principes psychologiques

- **Reactance reduction** : agree d'abord = le prospect arrete de se defendre.
- **Pain > gain** : "Human beings are more motivated to take action to get rid of pain than they are to achieve benefits."
- **Pattern interrupt par familiarite** : le prospect range mentalement le caller dans "in-network peer" et non "rando SDR".
- **Authority via specificity** : si tu connais le probleme dans le detail, le produit devient secondaire.
- **Control illusion** : "it's your call what happens after 30 sec" = ils gardent le pouvoir, donc baissent les armes.

### Exemples textuels (verbatim)

- Context leads pour Heard the Name :
  - "We work with a few other Sequoia portfolio companies."
  - "We work with partners in the Downtown LA office."
  - "We work with folks like Gong and Salesforce."
- Objection "Not interested" : *"Ah, my bad. You probably would've reached out if you needed help."*
- Objection "No budget" : *"Nowadays, it's hard enough to keep budget, let alone ask for something new."*
- Objection "Has competitor" : *"Honestly, most of the time it doesn't make sense to switch when you've got something in place."*

### Discovery questions (cold call mode)

Pas de discovery profonde sur le cold call — c'est un faux-ami. Une seule "incitative" question apres l'agree d'une objection. Format multiple-choice pour minimiser l'effort :
- *"Just so no one calls you again, is it that you're out of budget for this fiscal year, or you get put through the wringer every time you try to buy something?"*
- *"Is it more X or more Y?"* (force un choix, donc du talk time).

### Objection handlers — Mr. Miyagi Method (3 steps universels)

1. **Agree with objection** — "I hear ya, [reframe l'objection avec leur logique]."
2. **Incentivize conversation** — question multiple-choice pour relancer.
3. **Sell the test drive** — "You're probably not gonna buy this now. But [petit ask] so you at least know what's out there."

Le livre applique cette structure a 18 objections recurrentes ("send me an email", "we're all set", "call me in 6 months", "I'm not the right person", etc.).

### Voicemail templates

Strategie **Double Tap** : laisser au max 2 voicemails par sequence.
- **VM #1 (15 sec)** : context only, ton casual, redirige vers email.
- **VM #2 (30 sec)** : context + social proof.
Regles : lead with context (pas "Hi this is X from Y"), max 20 sec, tone "buddy laissant un message", number twice si CTA call-back.

### Metriques / benchmarks

- Connect rate moyen : 5% — top quartile : **13%**.
- Set rate moyen : 5% — top quartile : **16%**.
- Show rate moyen : 56% — top quartile : **72%**.
- Top quartile reps bookent **4x plus de meetings** que la moyenne.
- Volume cible : **155 dials/semaine** (~620/mois).
- Top 10% : **1 meeting pour 3 connects**.
- Repartition de la journee : Green Hours (8h-12h) = prospection seule ; Yellow Hours (12-15h) = meetings + catch-up ; Red Hours (15-18h) = admin.

### Citations

- *"The first words that come out of your mouth determine whether or not the prospect listens to anything else you say."*
- *"The key to cold calling is to get them to forget that it's a cold call."*

## Source 2 — Bad-Ass B2B Selling / Josh Braun (cours + corpus LinkedIn 2020-2026)

Pas un livre mais un corpus de cours (Braun Sales Academy, "Poke the Bear Cold Calling") + une bibliotheque LinkedIn ultra-citee. Approche **anti-pitch, low-pressure, curiosite-driven**. Ideal pour cible founders meta-allergiques au vendeur agressif.

### Framework principal

Ancien framework 4 etapes : **Permission -> Problem -> Poke the Bear -> Promise**. Braun a depuis evolue contre le "permission opener" generique ("Hi, do you have 27 seconds?") qu'il considere brule. Framework actuel pour cold calls : **Permission contextuelle -> Verification -> Context -> Problem ID -> Labeling -> Poke the Bear -> Tease**.

### Structure de script (Poke the Bear, 2023-2026)

1. **Permission contextuelle** : *"Hi Pete, Beth Jones with CaptivateIQ. We've never spoken before. And I know you weren't expecting my call, but I was hoping you could help me out for a moment."* ("help" = magique pour declencher reciprocite chez fonctions admin/founder).
2. **Verification** : *"To make sure I'm in the right place, are you involved in calculating commissions at ACME?"*
3. **Context** : *"We're working with sales teams with over 70 reps, and I came across your name."*
4. **Problem Identification** (question neutre, pas leading) : *"Is calculating and running commissions a manual process involving spreadsheets, or is it automated?"*
5. **Labeling** (Voss-style) : *"Sounds like you're getting data out of Salesforce, hard-pasting Excel pages into Google Sheets and then manually making adjustments."*
6. **Poke the Bear** (challenge soft, reference benchmark) : *"It takes most teams a few days to calculate and send statements to reps. How does that compare with your experience?"*
7. **Tease** (curiosite, pas pitch) : *"We help teams calculate error-free commission statements in a few hours using a different approach."*

### Principes psychologiques

- **Status quo bias** : la concurrence n'est jamais l'autre fournisseur, c'est l'inertie. "Shine a light on the cost of doing nothing."
- **Reactance theory** : zero pitch direct, zero leading question. Le prospect doit *se* convaincre.
- **Reciprocity** : demander "help" plutot que "time" desarme.
- **Curiosity gap** : le tease laisse un blank que le prospect doit combler.
- **No agenda posture** : Josh insiste — "Be willing to walk away." La detache emotionnelle augmente la conversion.

### Exemples textuels

- Cold call PPP/ERC : *"We're seeing that a lot of employers with less than 30 employees who have gone through one or two rounds of PPP are potentially missing out on ERC credits typically missed by general accounting firms, so I was hoping I could ask you a couple of quick questions to see if this might be relevant."*
- Reponse a "We already have a vendor" : *"Makes sense — most teams we work with did. Out of curiosity, if [pain X] still happens occasionally, is that something your current solution addresses, or you've just accepted it?"*

### Discovery questions

Neutres, pas leading. Format : "Are you doing X, or Y, or something else entirely?" — toujours laisser une troisieme option ouverte. Eviter "don't you think it would be great if…"

### Objection handlers

Pattern **Validate -> Disarm -> Pivot** :
- "We're all set" -> *"Totally fair. Most folks I call are. Quick question while I have you — [neutral problem question]?"*
- "Send me an email" -> *"Happy to. So I send something relevant and not generic, what's the one thing that would actually be worth your time to read?"*

### Voicemail

Style Braun : pas de pitch. *"Hi [name], it's Josh. I was calling about [specific thing]. Not sure if it's relevant — figured I'd ask. Number is [X]. No worries either way."* — la phrase "no worries either way" baisse la garde (low-pressure signal).

### Metriques

Josh ne publie pas de benchmarks chiffres systematiques. Insiste sur **call quality > volume**, **conversation rate**, et un focus sur "are you having more conversations this week than last week?"

### Citations

- *"Ditch the pitch. Poke the bear."*
- *"The best salespeople are the most detached."*

## Source 3 — Fanatical Prospecting / Jeb Blount (2015, ref Wiley)

Bible du volume + discipline. Plus "old-school inside sales", utile pour la mentalite et les voicemails, moins pour les scripts modernes B2B services.

### Framework principal

**Five-Step Telephone Prospecting Framework** :
1. **Get their attention** (use name, no "how are you doing today?").
2. **Identify yourself and your company** vite et clair.
3. **Tell them why you're calling** — reason for the call, ultra-specific.
4. **Bridge to a "because" statement** — pourquoi *eux* (Cialdini's "because" principle).
5. **Ask for what you want** (the meeting), assumptive.

### Structure de script

Script exemple Blount (verbatim) : *"This is Jeb with Acme Kitchen Supplies. The reason I am calling is because I read in the paper you are building a restaurant over on the 44 bypass. I wanted to learn more about your process for purchasing kitchen equipment supplies… when we get our design team working with your team before you make critical decisions about kitchen layout, you'll have more options in layout. You can often save a ton of money in construction costs and future labor costs with a more efficient and streamlined kitchen."*

### Principes psychologiques

- **Law of Replacement** : remplacer pipeline en continu, jamais attendre que ca seche.
- **30-Day Rule** : ce que tu prospectes aujourd'hui = pipeline dans 30/60/90 jours.
- **Disrupt the auto-pilot** : prospects sont en mode "no" automatique. Le nom + ton confiant + raison specifique cassent le pattern.
- **Assumptive close** : ne jamais demander "would you have time?" — toujours "Tuesday at 2 or Thursday at 10?"

### Discovery questions

Pas de discovery sur cold call selon Blount — l'objectif unique = meeting. Discovery = sur la prochaine etape.

### Objection handlers

Blount enseigne **Ledge -> Disrupt -> Ask Again** :
1. Ledge (acknowledge) : *"I get that…"*
2. Disrupt avec une raison contextuelle.
3. Ask again pour le meeting avec assumptive.

Exemple "I'm busy" : *"I figured you would be — that's exactly why I called instead of emailing. 15 minutes Tuesday or Thursday?"*

### Voicemail

Approche 5 steps :
1. Identify yourself.
2. Reason for call (specific).
3. Value statement (1 sentence).
4. Repeat your number twice slowly.
5. Promise email follow-up.
Cible : 25-30 sec max.

### Metriques

- Recommande 100+ dials/jour pour SDR full-time.
- Concept du "Golden Hours" : 8-11h et 14-17h.

### Citations

- *"The pipe is life."*
- *"The worst number in sales is one."* (1 deal, 1 prospect, 1 source).

## Source 4 — Never Split the Difference / Chris Voss (2016)

Pas un livre cold call mais la base psychologique de **toutes** les ecoles modernes (Braun, 30MPC, Sales Odyssey). Tactical empathy applique en 4 outils.

### Framework principal

**Tactical Empathy** : comprendre l'emotion + le sous-jacent pour augmenter l'influence. 4 leviers operationnels en cold call :

1. **Mirror** — repeter les 1-3 derniers mots du prospect, ton montant curieux.
2. **Label** — nommer l'emotion observee : "It sounds like…", "It seems like…", "It looks like…" (jamais "I think you feel").
3. **Calibrated questions** — questions "how" / "what", jamais "why" (perçu accusateur). Cree l'illusion de controle chez l'autre.
4. **No-oriented questions** — formuler pour obtenir un "no" qui declenche action : *"Is now a bad time?"* convertit mieux que *"Do you have a minute?"*

### Structure de script (application cold call)

Stack typique : **Mirror -> Label -> Calibrated Question**.
- Prospect : "We're using HubSpot right now and it's fine."
- Mirror : *"…it's fine?"*
- Label : *"It sounds like there are things you'd want to change but it's not painful enough to switch."*
- Calibrated Q : *"What would have to be true for you to consider another approach?"*

### Principes psychologiques

- **Amygdala hijack interrupt** : labeller une emotion negative la reduit (neurosciences citees par Voss).
- **Illusion of control** : calibrated questions forcent l'autre a resoudre, donc il s'engage cognitivement.
- **"That's right" > "You're right"** : objectif = leur faire dire "that's right" = vrai accord ; "you're right" = compliance polie sans buy-in.
- **"No" liberateur** : un "no" precoce baisse les defenses ; "yes" precoce active la suspicion.

### Discovery questions (calibrated)

- *"How am I supposed to do that?"* (objection inverse, force prospect a aider).
- *"What's the biggest challenge you face?"*
- *"What about this is important to you?"*
- *"How can we solve this problem?"*

### Objection handlers

- "Send me an email" -> *"Is sending you an email going to actually move this forward, or is it more a polite no?"* (no-oriented).
- "Not now" -> Label : *"It sounds like the timing feels off."* + calibrated : *"What would have to change for this to be worth 15 min?"*

### Metriques

Pas de metriques cold call (livre negociation). Donnees Voss : labels reduisent intensite emotionnelle mesurable en negociation FBI.

### Citations

- *"He who has learned to disagree without being disagreeable has discovered the most valuable secret."*
- *"Labels disarm. They take negative thoughts and externalize them."*

## Source 5 — Smart Calling (8e edition, 2020) / Art Sobczak

Approche **"smart" vs "cold"** : zero cold pur. Pre-call intelligence systematique. Tres applicable pour cibles founders / agence (recherche LinkedIn + site = 30 sec).

### Framework principal

**4-part Smart Calling Process** :
1. **Possible Value Proposition (PVP)** — phrases pre-construites mentionnant les benefices possibles que d'autres clients similaires ont obtenu. "Possible" = humble, evite resistance.
2. **Intelligence Gathering** — 5 min de recherche min : LinkedIn, news, site, social proof.
3. **Opening Statement** — combine PVP + intelligence + permission douce.
4. **Questioning + Listening** — 75% talk time prospect.

### Structure de script

- **Opening Statement type** : *"[Name], this is Art Sobczak with [company]. I noticed [specific trigger from research]. The reason I called is, companies like yours often see [PVP — possible value], and I wanted to ask you a few quick questions to see if it might apply to your situation. Do you have a couple minutes?"*

### Principes psychologiques

- **"People love to buy but hate to be sold"** — fondation philosophique.
- **"People buy for their reasons, not yours"** — donc questions > pitch.
- **Possible** comme softener majeur — reduit reactance, ouvre dialogue.

### Discovery questions

Sobczak insiste sur questions **specifiques + ouvertes** :
- *"How are you currently handling [process]?"*
- *"What would you change about it if you could?"*
- *"What's the impact of [problem] on your team?"*

### Objection handlers

Pattern **Acknowledge + Question** :
- "Not interested" -> *"OK — just to be sure I'm not missing something, what specifically are you not interested in — looking at alternatives, or [process]?"*

### Voicemail

Sobczak : VM = "teaser pour rappel" pas une vente. Format : nom + entreprise + raison specifique liee a leur situation + nom + numero.

### Metriques

Pas de benchmarks publics chiffres dans le livre. Insiste sur **call objectives primary + secondary** pour eliminer rejet.

### Citations

- *"People love to buy but hate to be sold."*
- *"You will never be 'cold' again."* (titre marketing).

## Source 6 — New Sales. Simplified. / Mike Weinberg (2012)

Reference pour **construire le message** plus que pour le script verbal. Le "Power Statement" est l'asset central reutilise partout (email, cold call, pitch, voicemail).

### Framework principal

**Power Statement** en 5 parts :
1. **Headline** — qui on est, qui on sert (1-2 phrases).
2. **Transitional phrase** — "Companies turn to us when…"
3. **Client Issues** — 3-7 problemes resolus, langage client (pas jargon).
4. **Offerings** — description ultra-courte de l'offre.
5. **Differentiators** — 5 raisons d'etre different.

Bits and pieces du Power Statement sont injectes en cold call, voicemail, email.

### Structure de script

Weinberg refuse les "canned scripts" mais impose un **plan**. Cold call type :
- Intro courte
- Pull une "client issue" du Power Statement
- Transition vers question
- Ask le meeting

### Principes psychologiques

- **Customer-issue language** : parler de leurs problemes dans leurs mots, pas en jargon vendeur.
- **Outcome over feature** : "specific outcomes" jamais "features".

### Discovery questions

Sur cold call : limite. Sur premier meeting : profonde. Cold call sert juste a obtenir ce meeting.

### Objection handlers

Approche directe, peu formalisee : recadrer sur le specific issue resolved + ask again.

### Citation

- *"Most salespeople describe what they do rather than why the customer should care."*

## Source 7 — Pick Up the Phone and Sell / Alex Goldfayn (2021)

Approche differente : moins SDR pur, plus AE / **comptes existants + prospects warm**. Format **proactive calls de 5-10 min**, volume 3-5/jour, ask direct.

### Framework principal

**Proactive Call Framework** :
1. Identifier qui appeler (client existant cross-sell, prospect dormant, referral).
2. Reason-for-call specifique.
3. Question d'ouverture qui invite a parler.
4. Silence (le client doit parler 75%+).
5. Ask direct ("When can I expect the PO?").

### Structure de script

- *"Hi [name], it's Alex. I was thinking about you because [specific reason]. How are things going with [project / area]?"*
- Apres reponse : silence, ecoute, puis ask direct.

### Principes psychologiques

- **Silence as a tool** — utiliser le silence dans chaque conversation.
- **Just ask** — la plupart des sales ne demandent jamais explicitement. Asking explicitly augmente le close rate massivement.
- **Proactive > reactive** — la plupart des deals manques viennent de l'absence d'appel.

### Discovery questions

- *"What are you working on right now where I could help?"*
- *"What's keeping you up at night?"*
- *"What didn't I ask that I should have?"*

### Objection handlers

Approche conversationnelle, peu de scripts formalises.

### Metriques

3-5 proactive calls/jour suffisent. Chaque call : 5-10 min. Goldfayn revendique doublement de revenus chez clients formes.

### Citation

- *"The customer should talk at least 75% of the time."*

## Source 8 — The Sales Development Playbook / Trish Bertuzzi (2016)

Reference **strategie SDR + cadence**, plus que script vocal pur. Tres utile pour : structuration de la sequence, qualif fit, persistence.

### Framework principal

6 elements : **Strategy, Specialization, Recruiting, Retention, Execution, Leadership**. Pour cold call -> section Execution.

### Cadence recommandee

- **6 a 10 touches min** pour atteindre 50% des leads.
- **4+ touches doivent etre des appels** (phone irremplaçable).
- **Multi-channel obligatoire** : phone + email + LinkedIn + (optionnel) physical mail.

### Structure de script

Bertuzzi enseigne le **"buyer-based messaging"** : segmenter par persona, mapper les pain points specifiques, construire un script par segment. Pas un opener universel.

### Principes psychologiques

- **Persistence beats brillance** : 60%+ de leads atteignables ne le sont qu'apres 6+ touches.
- **Specialization** : SDR != AE != customer success. Ne pas demander a un closer de cold-caller.

### Metriques

- Min 6 attempts, recommande 8-12.
- Mix : 50% phone, 30% email, 20% social.

### Citation

- *"Pipeline is a numbers game until you make it a quality game."* [paraphrase, a verifier]

## Source 9 — SPIN Selling / Neil Rackham (1988)

Cadre **questioning** pour ventes complexes B2B. Pas pour cold call pur (Rackham le dit : SPIN = pour la discovery call, pas l'opener). Mais les **Implication Questions** sont injectables en cold call pour creer urgence.

### Framework principal

**SPIN** :
- **Situation** — contexte factuel ("Comment vous gerez X aujourd'hui ?").
- **Problem** — identifier dissatisfactions ("Qu'est-ce qui ne marche pas dans cette approche ?").
- **Implication** — escalader le cout du probleme ("Quel impact ca a sur Y ?", "Combien ca vous coute en temps ?").
- **Need-Payoff** — laisser le prospect formuler la solution ("Si on resolvait X, qu'est-ce que ca vous apporterait ?").

### Application cold call

Limiter a 1-2 questions max. Souvent : 1 Problem + 1 Implication suffit pour creer l'urgence necessaire au meeting.

Exemple : *"Quand vous calculez vos commissions manuellement, qu'est-ce que ca coute en heures par mois a votre equipe ?"* (Implication).

### Principes psychologiques

- **Self-discovery** : si le prospect verbalise la douleur, il s'engage.
- **Top performers : 4x plus de questions Implication** que les moyens (donnee Huthwaite, 35 000 calls analyses).

### Citation

- *"In major sales, traditional closing techniques actually decrease your chance of getting the order."*

## Source 10 — Combo Prospecting / Tony Hughes (2018)

Refere principalement la **strategie multi-touch** : le cold call ne marche jamais seul.

### Framework principal

- **Triple** = phone + voicemail + email, le tout en <2 minutes, sur le meme prospect, dans l'ordre.
- **Quad / Quint / Sextet** = Triple + LinkedIn / Twitter / message texte.
- **Cible** : 30 Triples / jour pendant 2h block dedie.

### Application

Le cold call est l'arme de **finition** dans une sequence omnicanale. La sequence emails+social "warm" le prospect ; le phone "casse" la resistance.

### Principes psychologiques

- **Pattern interrupt** dans le cerveau du prospect : 3 canaux dans la meme heure = "ce caller est serieux + insiste pour une raison".
- **Multiplier effect** : 3x response rate vs single channel.

### Metriques

Hughes annonce **3x response rate** mini en passant a Triples vs single-channel.

### Citation

- *"The phone is a weapon of mass disruption."*

## Source 11 — JBarrows / Filling the Funnel / John Barrows (cours)

Cours SaaS-orient mais framework solide reutilisable B2B services.

### Framework principal

**AIDA appliqué au cold call** :
1. **Attention** — 5 premieres secondes, intro carre.
2. **Interest** — question ou mini value prop specifique au role/industrie.
3. **Desire** — engagement, dialogue.
4. **Action** — ask = du temps, pas une demo. Petit + specifique + facile a accepter.

### Structure de script

- Opener "Maybe You Can Help Me" : *"This is John Barrows with JB Sales. Actually, maybe you can help me. Do you have a moment?"* — efficace gatekeepers + admin.
- Hook Relevance Approach : *"We are working with other VPs of Sales in your space on three things right now: pipeline quality, AI readiness, and rep ramp time. Which of those is most pressing for you?"* (rule of 3 + multi-choice).

### Principes psychologiques

- **5-second rule** : premiere phrase determine si tu as les 25 prochaines secondes.
- **Help framing** : mot magique chez interlocuteurs en mode admin.

### Discovery questions

Questions specifiques au role : *"What are your top 3 priorities for the quarter ?"* / *"Which of these three is most pressing for you ?"* (multi-choice force a parler).

### Objection handlers

Style direct, assumptive : *"I get that — but if I could show you in 10 min how [other VP] solved [X], would Tuesday at 2 work?"*

### Voicemail

Lead with **reason, not name**. Number twice. <20 sec.

### Citation

- *"Attention is the first 5 seconds. You either earn the next 25 or you don't."*

## Source 12 — Sandler (David Sandler + reseau ~1967, ref permanente)

Methode **psychologie-driven**, longtemps dominante. Concepts clefs reutilisables en cold call.

### Framework principal

**Upfront Contract (UFC)** + **Pain Funnel** + **Reverse Selling**.

### Upfront Contract (UFC)

Verbal agreement initial : duree, sujets, outcomes possibles incluant "no".

Script : *"I have 30 minutes for this. My goal is to see if we can help. If we are not the right fit, that is fine. I will tell you. I would just ask for the same honesty from you. Does that work?"*

En cold call, UFC condense : *"I'll take 60 seconds to tell you why I called. If it's relevant, we book 15 min. If not, I disappear. Fair?"*

### Pain Funnel (8 questions classiques)

1. Tell me more about that…
2. Can you be more specific? Give me an example.
3. How long has that been a problem?
4. What have you tried to do about that?
5. Did what you try work?
6. How much do you think that has cost you?
7. How do you feel about how much that has cost you?
8. Have you given up trying to deal with the problem?

Niveaux : Surface (symptomes) -> Business Impact (consequences) -> Emotional Pain (stakes personnels). Atteindre Level 3 = closing virtuellement assure.

### Principes psychologiques

- **Permission to say no** baisse les defenses (reactance theory).
- **Reverse selling** : posture conseil > posture vendeur. Le prospect "achete" plutot que d'etre "vendu".
- **Negative reverse** : exprimer le doute pour declencher l'envie ("Honestly, I'm not sure we're a fit for you — but tell me about X").

### Objection handlers

UFC bypass beaucoup d'objections en amont. Pour objections en cours : reformuler vers une calibrated question Sandler-style.

### Citation

- *"You can't close a sale that isn't qualified."*

## Source 13 — The Challenger Sale / Dixon & Adamson (2011)

Pas un livre cold call mais un **paradigme de message**. Insight reframe applicable a la Problem Proposition.

### Framework principal

**Teach -> Tailor -> Take Control**, avec presentation 6-step **Commercial Teaching** :
1. **Warmer** — contextualiser la pertinence.
2. **Reframe** — surprendre avec un insight contre-intuitif sur leur statu quo.
3. **Rational Drowning** — data qui supportent le reframe.
4. **Emotional Impact** — connecter au pain personnel.
5. **A New Way** — vision alternative (sans encore pitcher).
6. **Your Solution** — produit comme realisation de A New Way.

### Application cold call

Le **Reframe** est l'or pour la Problem Proposition. Exemple : *"Most agencies think their bottleneck is lead volume. The data on 200+ agencies we've analyzed shows the real bottleneck is meeting-to-close conversion, which actually gets worse as volume increases."* — challenge un croyance fondamentale en 10 sec.

### Principes psychologiques

- **Counterintuitive insight** : force la reflexion, casse l'auto-pilot.
- **Authority via data** : la statistique specifique vaut mille generalites.

### Citation

- *"Customers don't value 'great relationships' as much as great insights."*

## Source 14 — Closer Academy (FR) — C.L.O.S.E.R. (Hormozi, popularise FR par Closer Academy)

Framework popularise dans la sphere closing FR (Hormozi origin US). Plus dedie au closing call que cold call, mais reutilisable pour la phase "advance".

### Framework

**C.L.O.S.E.R.** : Clarify, Label, Overview, Sell, Explain, Reinforce.

### Application cold call

Limite — c'est un framework closing. Mais 2 etapes injectables :
- **Clarify** : *"Pourquoi vous prenez 60 secondes pour me parler ?"* (force le prospect a verbaliser interet).
- **Label** : reformuler la douleur (cf Voss).

Pour cold call B2B services 6k+, garder uniquement Clarify + Label en early stage et reserver le reste pour le RDV.

### Citation

- *"Sell the vacation, not the flight ticket."*

## Synthese transverse

### Frameworks dominants (ce qui revient partout)

| Element | Sources convergentes | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Context-first opener** (vs generique) | 30MPC, Braun, Barrows, Sobczak, Weinberg | Universel 2020-2026. Skip si pas de context = call mort. |
| **Problem-led pitch** (vs feature-led) | 30MPC, SPIN, Sandler, Challenger, Weinberg, Braun | Quasi-universel. Le seul desaccord : niveau d'agressivite. |
| **Agree -> Disarm -> Reopen pour objections** | 30MPC (Miyagi), Braun (Validate-Pivot), Voss (label-question), Blount (Ledge) | Universel. Les noms changent, la structure est identique. |
| **Vendre le test drive, pas le produit** | 30MPC, Braun, Sobczak, Barrows | Universel. L'ask en cold call = ask minuscule. |
| **Multi-channel cadence** | Bertuzzi, Hughes, Barrows | Universel. Phone seul = sous-optimal. 6-10 touches min. |
| **Calibrated questions + labels** | Voss, Braun, Sandler | Universel pour reduce reactance. |

### Desaccords entre ecoles

1. **Permission opener "Do you have a minute ?" : oui ou non ?**
   - Pour : Braun (initialement), Sandler (UFC), Sobczak.
   - Contre : 30MPC (sauf si tailored avec contexte), Braun actuel ("Hi, do you have 27 secs ?" = brule).
   - Verdict 2026 : permission seule = mort. Permission **tailored** avec context = vit.

2. **Volume vs qualite**
   - Volume-first : Blount (100+/jour), Cardone, Bertuzzi (cadences denses).
   - Qualite-first : Sobczak (Smart Calling), Goldfayn (3-5 proactive/jour), Braun.
   - Verdict pour ton offre (services 6k+) : **qualite-first**. Cible founders / agences = listes de ~50-200 comptes, recherche obligatoire, 30-60 dials/jour suffisent si bien cibles.

3. **Hard close (Cardone) vs Permission-based (Braun)**
   - Cardone : assumptive aggressif, "press for the close".
   - Braun : detached, "be willing to walk away".
   - Verdict B2B services high-ticket : **Braun gagne**. Hard close brûle la relation chez un founder qui n'achete pas un produit transactionnel.

4. **Script ferme (Cardone, vieux Sandler) vs Plan structure (Weinberg, Braun, 30MPC)**
   - Verdict : **plan structure + improvisation guidee** est l'etat de l'art. Script ferme = sonne robot.

5. **Discovery sur cold call : oui ou non ?**
   - Pour : Sandler (Pain Funnel), SPIN.
   - Contre : 30MPC ("cold call = un objectif = meeting"), Blount.
   - Verdict : **1-2 questions calibrated max** sur cold call. Discovery profonde = sur le RDV suivant.

### Top 5 principes universels (a injecter dans tout script)

1. **Context-first, generic-never** : les 5 premieres secondes doivent prouver que tu sais a qui tu parles.
2. **Problem > Product** : decrire le probleme du prospect avec specificite dangereuse, le produit devient evident.
3. **Reduire la reactance** : agree avant de defendre, label avant de questionner, demander "help" avant de demander "time".
4. **Ask minuscule en sortie** : jamais "demo de 30 min", toujours "15 min mardi pour voir si pertinent".
5. **Posture detachee** : "no" est OK, "send me an email" est OK — le sales presse perd, le sales serein gagne.

### Top 5 erreurs critiques a eviter

1. **Pitch en premiere phrase** ("Hi, I'm calling about our amazing platform that…") -> objection automatique.
2. **Permission generique** ("Do you have 27 secs ?") -> brule + signale SDR.
3. **Defendre l'objection** ("Oh but we're different because…") -> active mode combat.
4. **Demander une demo** ("Can I book a 30-min demo ?") -> ask trop gros pour un cold call.
5. **Skipper la recherche** ("I'll just dial 100 numbers") -> connect rate s'effondre, listening reduit, conversion morte.

### Application à l'agence (cible founders agences B2B / consultants 6k+ services)

Stack recommande pour generer un script :

1. **Opener** : Tailored Permission (30MPC) si recherche sur founder dispo, sinon Heard the Name si ecosysteme partage. **Jamais** Permission generique.
2. **Hook** : Problem Proposition (30MPC) — triggering problem specifique au founder d'agence, one-sentence solution differenciante.
3. **Engagement** : 1-2 calibrated questions (Voss) ou Poke the Bear neutre (Braun) pour creer dialogue.
4. **Optionnel discovery courte** : 1 Implication question (SPIN) si engagement.
5. **Objection** : Mr. Miyagi 3-step (Agree -> Incentivize -> Test drive) sur les 5-8 objections recurrentes.
6. **CTA** : 15 min specifique (Tuesday 10 or Thursday 2 — assumptive Blount) ou "directional sense" (Test Drive 30MPC).
7. **Voicemail** : Double Tap 30MPC (VM1 context only, VM2 context + social proof), max 20-25 sec.
